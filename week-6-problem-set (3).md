# Problem Set: Text Generation and Decoding

## Instructions

Show the main steps of your calculations. Use the natural logarithm, $\ln$, when a log probability is required. Unless otherwise stated, assume that probabilities shown at each generation step are conditional probabilities produced by an autoregressive language model.

## Problem 1. Greedy Search

At one generation step, a language model produces the following probability distribution for the next token:

| Token | Probability |
|---|---:|
| A | 0.10 |
| B | 0.45 |
| C | 0.30 |
| D | 0.15 |

1. Which token is selected by greedy search?
2. Calculate the natural log probability of the selected token. Round your answer to three decimal places.

---

## Problem 2. Sequence Probability

An autoregressive language model generates three tokens with the following conditional probabilities:

$$
P(w_1)=0.5,\qquad
P(w_2\mid w_1)=0.4,\qquad
P(w_3\mid w_1,w_2)=0.2
$$

1. Calculate the probability of the complete sequence $(w_1,w_2,w_3)$.
2. Calculate its natural log probability. Round your answer to three decimal places.
3. Briefly explain why log probabilities are convenient when comparing long generated sequences.

---

## Problem 3. Beam Search

Suppose beam search is performed with a beam size of $b=2$.

At the first generation step:

$$
P(A)=0.5,\qquad P(B)=0.3,\qquad P(C)=0.2
$$

At the second generation step, the conditional probabilities are:

| Previous token | X | Y | Z |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 0.2 | 0.5 | 0.3 |
| B | 0.6 | 0.1 | 0.3 |

1. Which two hypotheses are retained after the first generation step?
2. Calculate the sequence probabilities of all candidate sequences expanded from these two hypotheses.
3. Which two sequences remain in the beam after the second generation step?

## Problem 4. Greedy Search vs. Global Sequence Probability

Consider the following two-step generation problem.

At the first step:

$$
P(A)=0.6,\qquad P(B)=0.4
$$

At the second step:

$$
P(X\mid A)=0.5,\qquad P(Y\mid A)=0.5
$$

$$
P(X\mid B)=0.9,\qquad P(Y\mid B)=0.1
$$

If there is a tie, assume that the token appearing first in the table is selected.

1. Determine the sequence generated by greedy search.
2. Calculate the probabilities of all four possible two-token sequences.
3. Identify the sequence with the highest overall probability.
4. Does greedy search find the sequence with the highest overall probability? Briefly explain what this result illustrates.

## Problem 5. Temperature and Softmax

A language model produces logits

$$
z=[2,1]
$$

The temperature-adjusted softmax probability is

$$
P_i=\frac{\exp(z_i/T)}{\sum_j \exp(z_j/T)}
$$

1. Calculate the probabilities when $T=1$.
2. Calculate the probabilities when $T=0.5$.
3. Compare the two distributions. What happens to the distribution when the temperature is reduced?

Round probabilities to three decimal places.

## Problem 6. Top-k Sampling

At one generation step, the model produces the following probabilities, already sorted from highest to lowest:

$$
[0.35,\;0.25,\;0.20,\;0.10,\;0.06,\;0.04]
$$

Top-k sampling is applied with $k=3$.

1. Which probabilities remain in the candidate set?
2. Renormalise the probabilities of the retained tokens so that they sum to 1.
3. What happens to the other three tokens during this sampling step?

## Problem 7. Nucleus Sampling

At one generation step, the sorted token probabilities are

$$
[0.40,\;0.25,\;0.15,\;0.10,\;0.06,\;0.04]
$$

Nucleus sampling is applied with $p=0.80$. Use the smallest set of highest-probability tokens whose cumulative probability is at least $p$.

1. Determine the tokens included in the nucleus.
2. Renormalise their probabilities so that they sum to 1.
3. Explain one important difference between nucleus sampling and top-k sampling.
